The problem

Monitoring is still reactive, in a region that can't afford to be

Heavy rainfall, fragile terrain and unplanned hill cutting make the North Eastern Region especially prone to landslides, flash floods and slope failures.

Today

Risk is only checked after it happens

Vulnerable zones are inspected after reports come in, not watched continuously. Field information travels by phone call and word of mouth, with no shared record — so a blocked road can isolate a village for days before help arrives.

With Sentry.ai

A live model of every slope

Rainfall, soil moisture, satellite imagery and terrain data feed a model that's always running. Authorities see risk shift in real time, instead of finding out once a slope has already given way.

The solution

One system. Every vulnerable slope.

No manual inspection rounds and no waiting for a phone call. Sentry.ai watches continuously, scores risk as conditions change, and gets the result to the people who need to act on it — before the next heavy spell of rain.

How it works

Three stages, running continuously

01

Collect signals

Rainfall patterns, soil moisture sensors, satellite imagery, terrain/slope data and historical landslide records feed in continuously, sector by sector.

02

Score risk

AI/ML models trained on regional terrain and weather patterns identify high-risk zones and predict the likelihood of a landslide event.

03

Alert & respond

Real-time alerts reach district administrations, disaster management authorities and local communities, prioritised by severity.

Impact

Reach every slope before the rain does

Sentry.ai is built on a simple bet: disaster preparedness in the North Eastern Region shouldn't depend on how fast word travels after a slope has already failed. It should depend on how early the signal was caught — for less loss of life, less infrastructure damage, and more climate-resilient governance.
